勿体ないA Mottainai Manifesto

If we use a tree,
we use all of it.

Inspired by the Japanese principle of mottainai, a respect for resources and a refusal to waste.

In Japan, mottainai reflects responsibility toward materials. We apply that thinking to furniture.

Instead of taking only the trunk and discarding the rest, we design with the entire tree in mind: bark, branches, roots, leaves, stems.

Every part of a tree has value.
Every tree part has a purpose.

素材探究Material Exploration

Nothing is offcut.

Each part of the tree was tested as a material in its own right, from the leaves that fall to the fruit it bears.

試料
葉

Leaves become
bioplastic

Dried leaves, gelatin, glycerin, white vinegar and cornstarch were combined and heated until fully mixed, then poured into moulds and left to dry into a flexible structural plastic.

Ratios were varied to test strength, moulds added texture, and dyes from foraged berries and coffee created pattern and colour. Leaves soaked for three days in glycerin and water became a preserved, flexible material that could be sewn, with patterns drawn from traditional Japanese design.

幹

A second life
for old desks

The teak came from old physics desks. It was planed and cut to size, and since the design needed more thickness, two sections were glued and clamped into a single, stronger piece of wood.

考えIdeation
木

The chair's form was inspired by Japanese characters. The symbol for tree, 木, shaped the structure: its simple composition already resembles a stool.

The reference also carries the manifesto: one chair made from the whole tree, rather than from separate parts.

祖型Prototyping

Locked together,
without glue.

型

Joinery

Japanese wood joinery was the main inspiration, especially interlocking joints that need no glue. Several assemblies were developed to explore the approach.

Puzzle

Japanese puzzles became a second reference: a system where each piece locks into place, so that once fully assembled the chair is stable and self-supporting.

作成Making

From workshop
to makerspace.

過程
木工

The wooden base was cut, shaped and assembled in the Imperial College wood workshop, using the joinery techniques developed through the project. The cushion was made separately in the RCA Makerspace, working with the tools and materials available there to fabricate and refine the final form.
